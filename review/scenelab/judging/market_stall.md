# Market stall: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign scenes of this type, described from the scenes themselves. Westwood has only three.

**What it is for.** A trader's pitch: a striped awning to sell under, with the stock set out around it.

**What you notice first.** A large two-colour striped awning (purple and orange, or green and red) on its poles.

**What it holds.** Common pieces are the awning and its poles, barrels and water barrels, and iron-bound crates. Pieces seen sometimes are crates of apples, a trader's cart or an ore cart, an armour rack, torch poles, and a potion seller's bubbling cauldron with a fairy jar and a cushioned stool. A stump or a rock may sit at the edge. Westwood's stalls have no tables of goods, signs or crowds of baskets.

**How it is laid out.** The awning sits in the middle. The stock gathers around it in small clusters: barrels in a short row of three or four, a pair of apple crates, an iron crate on its own. The cart is parked at the side. A torch pole or two may stand at the edges. Each stall's goods fit its trade, so a potion seller has a cauldron and jar, and a provisioner has apples and barrels.

**Density and feel.** Compact and full. Eleven to sixteen pieces close together, with hardly any open ground inside the pitch. Half a dozen kinds is typical.

**Settings.** Two are in towns (one right beside a road) and one is in an icy northern settlement.

**What a designer's hand looks like here.** The stock tells you what is sold. Barrels are lined up as if just unloaded and the cart is left beside them. Each of Westwood's stalls is different in colour and in goods.
