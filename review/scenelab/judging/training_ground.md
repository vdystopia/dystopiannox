# Training ground: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign has no outdoor training ground at all, so this page describes the scene from its purpose and from the nearest things Westwood does show. The real pictures may be few or none.

**What it is for.** A practice ground where arms are trained with, with targets to strike and racks of weapons and armour close at hand.

**What you notice first.** In Westwood, the only outdoor things of this kind are rows of armour racks, sometimes with a stand of helmet poles. They appear inside war camps beside a road, not as a separate yard. A real practice ground would be anchored by its targets or by an open patch of beaten ground.

**What it holds.** The pieces closest to this in Westwood's war camps are armour racks in a group of three to six, helmet poles, a polearm rack, a bow rack, and barrels near by. Westwood has no outdoor archery targets, straw dummies, sparring rings or spectators' benches anywhere in its campaign exteriors.

**How it is laid out.** Westwood's racks stand in a short row, a zigzag or a tight group toward one side of the camp. The rest of the camp (fire, tents, stores) is kept apart from them. A practice ground built in the same spirit would keep its arms in a row along an edge and leave the middle open.

**Density and feel.** Westwood's rack rows are small and orderly, at most half a dozen pieces, in a camp of twenty to thirty. They read as military and tidy, in contrast to the rough look of the rest of the camp.

**Settings.** The racks appear in war camps on town maps beside roads, and once in the icy north, where four racks stand with a single pup tent.

**What a designer's hand looks like here.** Order where order belongs. Weapons and armour are lined up, everything else in the camp is looser, and the arms are kept to one side of the open ground.
