# Ogre camp: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign scenes of this type, described from the scenes themselves.

**What it is for.** A crude, brutish camp for cooking, eating and sleeping, with the leftovers of meals all around.

**What you notice first.** A large fire pit, lit or cold, with raw meat hanging on racks nearby. Often a carcass lies beside it.

**What it holds.** Common pieces are the fire pit, meat racks, huge log benches and squat stools, barrels, torch poles, and rock pillars and boulders. Pieces seen sometimes are a hanging carcass, leg and arm bones and skulls strewn on the ground, big sacks, a water barrel, a dead shrub, a great bed with a bearskin, a heavy table, a mound of tusks, and a skull mounted on a post. Westwood's ogre camps have no tents, racks of weapons, crates, or neat stores.

**How it is laid out.** The fire and its meat form the core. Benches and stools sit loosely around it, not in a neat ring. Bones and skulls lie in small spills a little way off. Sleeping things (the bed, the bearskin) and the table are set apart from the fire on their own. Barrels stand in a small group at one side. Rock pillars and boulders close off an edge of the scene. Torch poles stand at the edges of the bigger camps.

**Density and feel.** Middling. From five pieces to about thirty, with about a dozen typical, and a good many kinds for the size. Spread loosely, with open patches between groups. It feels messy, but deliberately so.

**Settings.** Mostly swamp, with one camp inside a castle's grounds. Several camps share one swamp, as clusters of a larger village.

**What a designer's hand looks like here.** The mess is deliberate. Bones lie where meals were eaten, not evenly sprinkled. Meat is kept by the fire, and the bed with its bearskin is set apart. Rocks and the swamp's edge shape where each cluster stops.
