# Guard post: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign has almost no outdoor guard post, so this page describes the scene from its purpose and from the qualities of Westwood's nearest scenes. The real pictures may be few or none.

**What it is for.** A lit watch-point at a gate, a bridge or the edge of a camp, from which a way in is watched at night.

**What you notice first.** For a post like this, the anchor would be a light: a standing brazier or a torch pole by the way it watches.

**What it holds.** Outdoors, the lights at the edge of Westwood's camps and yards are torch poles, standing alone; Westwood never sets up a light as a post with a seat, rack and water barrel round it. Where a brazier does appear, it stands at the edge of whatever group it lights, not as the centre of a furnished post.

**How it is laid out.** Westwood's torch poles stand singly at the edge of the place they light, a little off the way, not in the middle of it.

**Density and feel.** A real outdoor watch-point, as Westwood treats it, is minimal: one light and little else.

**Settings.** Lights at the edges of camps and yards occur in towns, forests and swamps, and in castle grounds.

**What a designer's hand looks like here.** Westwood lets one light do the job of marking a watched way. An elaborate furnished post is unlike anything in Westwood's campaign.
