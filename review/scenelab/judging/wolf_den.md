# Wolf den: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign scenes of this type, described by their qualities, not scene by scene. The pictures show the place only, with nothing living in it.

**What it is for.** A lair in the wild: bedding straw, the remains of meals, and the odd lost chest.

**What you notice first.** Scattered straw bedding on the ground, often with a small chest or two lying among it.

**What it holds.** Common pieces are straw (from a few heaps to a great litter) and small chests. Pieces seen sometimes are bones and skulls, rock pillars and boulders, small stones, barren shrubs, and a torch pole at the edge. Westwood's dens have no fire, tents, barrels, crates or furniture.

**How it is laid out.** The straw spreads in loose patches and drifts without rows, thickest in the middle of the lair and thinning toward its edges. The chests are dropped here and there among the straw, not stacked. Where there are bones, they lie in small spills at the margins of the bedding. Rock pillars and boulders stand at the edges and close the lair against the rock. A den may lie by a path or back onto a wall.

**Density and feel.** From a bare handful of pieces to a large den of several dozen, mostly straw and bones. It has very few kinds and the commonest piece dominates. It feels feral and untidy, with no human order.

**Settings.** Wild places: woods, wet ground, rocky hollows at a town's edge.

**What a designer's hand looks like here.** The litter is uneven, dense where the lair is used and thin at the margins. Chests hint at a reward and a story. Rocks are used as the lair's walls. Anything arranged in rows, or tidy stores, would be out of place in Westwood's dens.
