# Farmyard: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign scenes of this type, described by their qualities, not scene by scene.

**What it is for.** Fodder and bedding kept on a farmstead or in a work yard: heaps of straw and the barrels that go with them.

**What you notice first.** Heaps of straw piled loosely against a wall or a rock face.

**What it holds.** Every Westwood farmyard has straw heaps and a barrel or two. Common extras are a torch on the wall, boulders, and rock pillars. Now and then a yard has a small story piece: a stack of barrels, or the wreck of something that once stood there lying in the straw. Westwood's farmyards have no sacks of feed, hay carts, windmills, troughs, pitchforks or threshing floors.

**How it is laid out.** The straw lies in a loose drift of three to ten heaps, some touching, some a step apart, along the foot of a wall or cliff, following its line even where it curves. Barrels stand apart from the straw, in a pair or alone at one end. The torch is fixed to the wall above. Stones and boulders may close the far end.

**Density and feel.** Sparse to modest. Five to about twenty pieces of two to six kinds, with straw nearly always the commonest. It feels like a corner of rough working land: practical, a bit untidy, and lived against its wall.

**Settings.** Forest settlements and town outskirts, set back from the roads, often in a nook of rock or against a building.

**What a designer's hand looks like here.** The straw follows the wall or rock it is heaped against, not a grid. Barrels are kept a little apart from it. A small story touch makes a yard feel used. Westwood keeps these scenes simple, with no farm machinery.
