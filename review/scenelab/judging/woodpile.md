# Woodpile: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign scenes of this type, described from the scenes themselves. Westwood has only three, all in one forest, and none of them is a stacked woodpile, so the real pictures will be few.

**What it is for.** Wood for burning. In Westwood's forest this is not a worked stack but a fallen log lying where it dropped, in the undergrowth.

**What you notice first.** A single large fallen log lying on the forest floor.

**What it holds.** Each of Westwood's scenes has one fallen log and a few barren, leafless shrubs. Seen once each are a tall rock pillar and a hollow stump used as a hiding place. Westwood has no stacked woodpiles, chopping blocks, axes in stumps, sawhorses or neat cords of split wood in its campaign exteriors.

**How it is laid out.** The log lies alone, and the dead shrubs grow loosely around it at a little distance, some in a cluster of two or three. There are no rows and no alignment, just the forest floor. One log lies near a rock wall with shrubs along it, and another lies in the open with a hollow stump a few steps away.

**Density and feel.** Very sparse. Five to seven pieces, nearly all of them shrubs, with two or three kinds. It feels wild and untouched, a found corner of the woods rather than a place anyone tends.

**Settings.** Deep forest, away from roads and water.

**What a designer's hand looks like here.** A light touch. A single natural feature is placed so the eye finds it, framed by a few dead shrubs, and sometimes hides a small reward in a stump. Anything stacked, sawn or arranged in a row would be unlike Westwood's own scenes of this type.
