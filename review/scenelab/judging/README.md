# Westwood's exterior scenes: what real ones are like

For the blind judge: a general description of the outdoor scenes in Westwood's Nox campaign, true of every scene type. Each type has its own page in this folder. The pages describe the qualities real scenes of a type share, never any one scene: do not try to match a picture to a scene you think a page is about.

**Every scene has a purpose.** A real scene is where something is done: a camp, a burial ground, a household's crop, a landing. Its pieces are what that work needs, and each stands where it would be used.

**Zones, not scatter.** Pieces gather in a few small clusters with open ground between them. Things of a kind stand together: barrels touching or nearly so, crates in pairs, usually one kind of barrel or crate to a scene. Rows run along the diagonal wall lines and are rarely perfectly even.

**The site matters.** Scenes lean on something: a rock face, a castle or town wall, a fence, a road, a shore. Many pieces stand close against walls. Few scenes float alone in an empty glade, and those in the open usually sit beside a road.

**Density and variety.** Westwood is sparse more often than crowded: a handful of pieces for a small scene, a few dozen for a large camp. One or two kinds usually dominate. No two scenes of a type are laid the same way.

**Light.** Outdoors, light comes from torch poles, wall torches, fires, and now and then a brazier or street lamp. Candles appear only in a few castle chapel and cell corners, never in a camp, yard or garden.

**A designer's hand** shows in small irregularities with intent: a gap in a row, a piece a little off the line, a cluster shaped by a rock outcrop or the bend of a wall. Mechanical sameness and random strewing both look wrong.
