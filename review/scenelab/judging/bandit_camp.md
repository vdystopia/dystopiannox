# Bandit camp: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign scenes of this type, described by their qualities, not scene by scene.

**What it is for.** A band living rough. It is a place to sleep, cook, and keep stores and arms.

**What you notice first.** Westwood has two kinds. The *war camp* stands in the open beside a road. It has a fire ringed by small stones, a large striped awning or pavilion, and a single small tent. The *hideout* is tucked against rock. Cots stand against the rock face, lit by torches on the wall, with boulders and rock pillars about. A hideout often has no fire at all.

**What it holds.** Common pieces are barrels (the most frequent), iron-bound crates, small stones, boulders and tall rock pillars, wall torches and torch poles. War camps add a small group of armour racks with helmet poles, and stores (a cart, a crate of food). Sometimes there is a chest, a stool, a stump or two, straw, a chair and table, a cauldron, hung meat, a polearm rack, or tools of a trade. Westwood's camps have no bedrolls laid out in rows, no dummies or targets, and almost no seats around the fire.

**How it is laid out.** The pieces form small clusters with gaps between them. Barrels huddle in a knot of three to six. The racks stand in a short row or a tight group toward one side, and the awning sits apart from the fire. The fire's stones form a tight ring. Hideouts follow the rock: cots singly or in pairs along the wall, stores in the corners.

**Density and feel.** A camp has anywhere from a handful of pieces to about forty, typically around twenty.

**Settings.** Beside roads at a town's edge, forest clearings and rock hollows; now and then somewhere less expected.

**What a designer's hand looks like here.** Rocks frame the camp: a ring of stones around the fire, pillars at its edge, boulders closing a side. The rhythm is uneven, a cluster is set off-centre, and the camp uses the cliff or road it sits by.
