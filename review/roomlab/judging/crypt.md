# Crypt: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign rooms of this type, described from the rooms themselves.

## What it is for

Where the dead are laid. Westwood's crypts are the stone vaults of a town's catacombs, a chain of small chambers, so many have two to four doorways and read as rooms you pass through; a few have no ordinary door at all.

## What you notice first

A stone sarcophagus, or a matched pair. In most crypts the sarcophagi stand free on the floor, in a pair or a short row of two to six, squarely aligned with each other and with the room. In one town's catacomb they lie along a wall instead, two side by side.

## What it holds

- Sarcophagi in about four crypts in five; tombstones in about half, often one close at the head of a sarcophagus.
- A crypt chest in about a quarter, against a wall or near the tombs.
- Sometimes: statues (one chamber has eight round its walls among its tombstones), an obelisk or three among the tombs.
- Some chambers hold nothing but two to six tombstones.
- Not in Westwood's crypts: furniture, shelves, barrels, sacks, tapestries, carpets, plants or columns.

## How it is arranged

Orderly and nearly symmetrical. The tombs keep to the room's axes, in pairs or short rows with walking room between them; the middle is theirs. The walls are mostly bare; when used, they carry a chest, a pair of sarcophagi or a ring of statues. The way in from a door is clear floor.

## Density and feel

Sparse and quiet. Most crypts hold one to three kinds of thing, with about three quarters of the floor open stone. Most have a light or two; some are unlit. The chambers are small, about 25 to 90 tiles.

## Variants

The campaign's crypts are vaults beneath the human towns' graveyards; the odd obelisk among the tombs is the only touch of another style.

## What a designer's hand looks like here

Restraint and repetition. The same chamber layout recurs from vault to vault along a catacomb, so sister rooms look alike. Within a chamber the variety is small: a sarcophagus with its tombstone, a chest, a pair of statues. The rare showpiece, the chamber ringed with statues or the tombs among obelisks, is one bold idea, not a scatter of extras.
