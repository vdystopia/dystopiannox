# Hall: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign rooms of this type, described from the rooms themselves.

Westwood's campaign has three halls (a town's, Dun Mir's and one in the Land of the Dead). The other Westwood rooms on the sheet may be of the kin types: chapel, gallery, great hall, shrine and throne room.

## What it is for

A hall of state or passage with no seat to face: an antechamber, an outer hall.

## What you notice first

- The town hall (128 tiles): two dozen statues and as many columns lined along two opposite walls.
- The Dun Mir hall (92 tiles, six doors): almost nothing; two victory statues and four hanging shields on the back walls.
- The Land of the Dead hall (209 tiles): six mana obelisks and four of its columns standing in a wide bare room.

## What it holds

- Common: statues or obelisks; columns; hangings (shields) in Dun Mir.
- Sometimes: a chest.
- Not seen: tables, chairs, benches, plants, rugs, shelves, stores, beds.

Each hall holds only two to four kinds of thing.

## How it is arranged

The middle is open in all three. In the town and Dun Mir halls the pieces keep to the walls; the town hall mirrors one long wall in the other, statues and columns in long lines. In the Land of the Dead hall the obelisks and columns stand out from the walls in a symmetric pattern, the centre left clear. The way between the doors runs clear down the middle.

## Density and feel

Open and stately: the emptiest Dun Mir hall is almost all bare floor, the others about seven to nine tenths open.

## Variants

- Town: plain statues and columns.
- Dun Mir: victory statues and Dun Mir shields.
- Land of the Dead: mana obelisks and dark columns.

## The kin rooms on the sheet

- Throne rooms: a throne against a back wall facing a long open floor; statues, columns or obelisks, tapestries.
- Shrines: four obelisks round a book or holy thing. The gallery: paintings, tapestries, plants, orreries. The chapel: rows of benches, columns, tapestries. Great halls: tables, benches, hearths, shields.

## What a designer's hand looks like here

Westwood repeats one piece freely in a hall (two dozen statues) in strict symmetry, or leaves a hall nearly bare with a few pieces on one wall. Variety is low; character comes from the walls and the scale of the open floor.
