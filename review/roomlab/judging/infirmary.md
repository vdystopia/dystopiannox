# Infirmary: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign rooms of this type, described by their qualities, not room by room.

## What it is for

A sick room, where the wounded are nursed: beds for the patients and the healer's remedies. No room in Westwood's campaign nurses the sick, so a sheet may show rooms of the kin work types (a herbalist's room, a kitchen, a laboratory, a library, a smithy or a winch room). Judge each picture as a room of its own type, by its design. What follows is what an infirmary is, and what Westwood's nearest rooms show.

## What you notice first

In an infirmary, the row of cots. Westwood's own rows of beds are in its barracks: beds or cots of one kind side by side along one wall, sometimes with chests beside them. In its herbalists' rooms the eye goes to the potion shelves and the desk, or to a fire.

## What it holds

An infirmary would hold cots of one kind, the healer's remedies and a table. Westwood's herbalists hold potion shelves, bookcases, shelves of jars, a desk, sometimes an alchemist's desk, a table with chairs, a fairy jar, and a stray piece of stock (a barrel, a crate); some have a fire with its tools and a rug before it.

## How it is arranged

In Westwood's herbalists' and other work rooms the pieces keep mostly to the walls, three or four walls used, with one table group standing out in the room and the rest of the middle open. The barracks put their row of beds against one wall, side by side, and the table elsewhere.

## Density and feel

Westwood's herbalists are balanced: about ten kinds of thing, with roughly two thirds of the floor open. They are not crowded.

## Variants

Westwood has no culture variants of the type.

## What a designer's hand looks like here

Mixed, practical furnishing: a writing desk among bookcases and shelves of jars, a spittoon by the table, a rug by the fire. Walls are used unevenly, one lined with shelves, another with a single piece.
