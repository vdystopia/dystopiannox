# Infirmary: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign rooms of this type, described from the rooms themselves.

## What it is for

A sick room, where the wounded are nursed: beds for the patients and the healer's remedies. No room in Westwood's campaign nurses the sick, so there is no real infirmary to describe. The real rooms on the sheet will be of the kin work types (a herbalist's room, a kitchen, a laboratory, a library, a smithy or a winch room) and should be judged as those. What follows is what an infirmary is, and what Westwood's nearest rooms show.

## What you notice first

In an infirmary, the row of cots. Westwood's own rows of beds are in its barracks: four beds in a row along one wall, or six cots in a row with a chest at each. In its herbalists' rooms the eye goes to the potion shelves and the alchemist's desk, or to a freestanding fire.

## What it holds

An infirmary would hold cots of one kind, the healer's remedies and a table. Westwood's herbalists hold potion shelves (one or two), bookcases, shelves of jars, a desk and an alchemist's desk, a table with chairs, a fairy jar, and odd stores: an apple crate, a barrel, a crate. One has a bearskin before its fire, with the bellows beside it.

## How it is arranged

In Westwood's herbalists' and other work rooms the pieces keep mostly to the walls, three or four walls used, with one table group standing out in the room and the rest of the middle open. The barracks put their row of beds against one wall, side by side, and the table elsewhere.

## Density and feel

Westwood's herbalists are balanced: ten to twelve kinds of thing in 50 to 70 tiles, with roughly two thirds of the floor open. Neither is crowded.

## Variants

Westwood has no culture variants of the type. Of the two herbalists, one is in Dun Mir and one in the ogre-held fortress.

## What a designer's hand looks like here

Mixed, practical furnishing: a writing desk and an alchemist's desk among five bookcases and two shelves of jars, a spittoon by the table, a bearskin by the fire. Walls are used unevenly, one lined with shelves, another with a single piece.
