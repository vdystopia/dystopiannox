# Shop: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign rooms of this type, described by their qualities, not room by room.

Westwood's shops are nearly all town shops.

## What it is for

A trader sells over a desk. Each shop keeps one trade: a potion and magic shop, a general store, or an armourer's.

## What you notice first

The trader's desk, in nearly every shop (a big one may have two, a small one none). In about half it stands near a back wall; in the others, more often the large ones, out on the floor. Then the goods along the walls.

## What it holds

- Potion and magic shops: potion shelves, often several side by side; glowing fairy jars; bookcases; now and then a cauldron, a stove, a keg or a stool.
- General stores: steel crates and steel barrels, clustered by the walls; a barrel of tools; a chair by the desk.
- Armourers: pole-arm racks, armour and clothes racks, bow and quiver racks, crossbows and swords hung on the walls, crossed weapons, a shield, trader's shelves, a few barrels; now and then an ornament.
- A small shop may mix trades: a rack or two, a shelf of potions, an apple crate, a painting.
- Not seen: plants, tables with seats, beds, hearths, rugs.

## How it is arranged

Almost everything stands against the walls; in half the shops nothing at all stands in the middle. Goods gather in short clusters of one piece (potion shelves together, crates in a heap) rather than spread evenly. Only in large armourers do racks stand out on the floor.

## Density and feel

Balanced: most shops leave a little more than half the floor open, the middle clear to walk to the desk; a small general store may be packed with crates and barrels. Five to ten kinds of thing in a room.

## Variants

Each culture brings its own racks, shelves and walls; the trades are the same everywhere.

## What a designer's hand looks like here

Odd lived-in pieces: a cauldron or a stove in a magic shop, a painting, an ornament, a chair beside the desk. The desk is seldom centred, and the stock is heaped toward one wall or corner, leaving another nearly bare.
