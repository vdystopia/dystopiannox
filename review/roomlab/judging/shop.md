# Shop: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign rooms of this type, described from the rooms themselves.

Westwood's campaign has eight shops, seven in towns and one in Dun Mir.

## What it is for

A trader sells over a desk. Each shop keeps one trade: a potion and magic shop, a general store, or an armourer's.

## What you notice first

The trader's desk, in seven of the eight shops (a big armourer's has two; one small potion shop has none). In about half it stands near a back wall; in the others, including both large armourers, out on the floor. Then the goods along the walls.

## What it holds

- Potion and magic shops: potion shelves, three to five, often three side by side; glowing fairy jars; bookcases; in one an animated cauldron, in another a stove, a cider keg, an obelisk and a crude stool.
- General stores: steel crates and steel barrels, clustered by the walls; a barrel of tools; a chair by the desk.
- Armourers: pole-arm racks, armour and clothes racks, bow and quiver racks, crossbows and swords hung on the walls, crossed weapons, a shield, trader's shelves, a few barrels; in the Dun Mir one a statue vase.
- One small mixed shop: quiver racks, an apple crate, potion shelves, a helm shelf, a painting.
- Not seen: plants, tables with seats, beds, hearths, rugs.

## How it is arranged

Almost everything stands against the walls; in half the shops nothing at all stands in the middle. Goods gather in short clusters of one piece (three potion shelves together, crates in a heap of four to seven) rather than spread evenly. Only in the two large armourers do racks stand out on the floor, about a third of the pieces.

## Density and feel

Balanced: most shops leave a little more than half the floor open, the middle clear to walk to the desk. The exception is a cramped general store of 32 tiles packed with crates and barrels. Five to ten kinds of thing in a room.

## Variants

Dun Mir's single shop is an armourer's; the rest are town shops.

## What a designer's hand looks like here

Odd lived-in pieces: a bubbling cauldron or a stove in a magic shop, a painting, a statue vase, a chair beside the desk. The desk is seldom centred, and the stock is heaped toward one wall or corner, leaving another nearly bare.
