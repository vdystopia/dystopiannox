# Throne room: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign rooms of this type, described from the rooms themselves.

Westwood's campaign has four throne rooms: Hecubah's in Dun Mir (492 tiles) and three in the Land of the Dead (the Lich Lord's at 356 tiles, the finale's at 296, and a niche of 84). None is a town's. Halls, the kin type, may also stand on the sheet.

## What it is for

Where a ruler sits in state and is approached across a long floor.

## What you notice first

The throne, against a back wall, facing down the room toward the doors. Hecubah's is the Dun Mir throne on its base with its high back; the lich's is a dark throne on a base with its shadow.

## What it holds

- Dun Mir: the throne, four wolf statues flanking it on its wall, two victory statues, two Dun Mir columns, flame basins in pairs along a runner to the doors.
- The Lich Lord's: fourteen tapestries round the walls, six columns (three along each side wall), two pairs of judgement balances, four mana obelisks, two arks on the throne's wall, two tombstones.
- The finale's: little but the throne, four mana obelisks, five tapestries and two chests.
- The niche: the throne, four mana obelisks in pairs, two tombstones.
- Not seen: tables, chairs, benches, plants, beds, shelves, stores, hunting trophies.

## How it is arranged

The long floor before the throne is left open. In the Lich Lord's room and the niche everything stands at the walls, columns lining the side walls, and the room is symmetric about the throne. Hecubah's and the finale's are looser: some pieces stand out on the floor (the flame basins along the runner, obelisks), and the arrangement is not mirrored.

## Density and feel

Among the emptiest rooms in the campaign: four fifths to over nine tenths open floor, five to eight kinds of thing. Processional and vast.

## The kin rooms on the sheet

Halls: open rooms with statues or obelisks and columns along the walls, or a few shields and statues on the back walls.

## What a designer's hand looks like here

Huge rooms left almost bare; a throne built of several pieces; one kind of hanging repeated many times round the walls; odd ritual pieces (balances, arks, tombstones) set for effect. Where Westwood repeats a piece it is a hanging, not a crowd of furniture.
