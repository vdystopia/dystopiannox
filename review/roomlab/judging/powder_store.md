# Powder store: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign rooms of this type, described from the rooms themselves.

Westwood's campaign has only one true powder store, so most of the real rooms on the sheet will be its kin: cellars, storerooms and a treasury. Judge them as Westwood's stores in general.

## What it is for

A magazine for black powder in a keep or a mine, kept apart from the living rooms. It is a store, nothing more.

## What you notice first

The black-powder kegs. In Westwood's one powder store, eleven of them fill a 30-tile room.

## What it holds

- Common: powder kegs, and almost nothing else. Two plain barrels stand among them.
- Not seen: lights of any kind (no candelabra, no brazier), beds, tables, chairs, shelves, rugs, plants.
- In the kin stores: plain kegs, a great cask, piled barrels, dark crates, steel crates, barrels of tools, a chest; the ogres' hoard has their chests, sacks of gold, a great cask and a spittoon.

## How it is arranged

The kegs stand in knots and short rows, and about a third of them stand free of the walls, not lined up neatly against them. The layout is close to mirror-symmetric. Westwood's cellars push their kegs into one or two corners or a tight block, with crates by the door. Storerooms mix crates and barrels along two or three walls and leave a lane.

## Density and feel

Crowded and practical. The powder store has little open floor, with kegs in most of it. The cellars run from a tight block in a small room to a few kegs left in the corners of a big, mostly empty undercroft.

## Variants

Only the town or keep style. The kin include a Dun Mir storeroom (a Dun Mir chest, apple crates, barrels) and the ogres' hoard.

## What a designer's hand looks like here

- Kegs heaped in knots, a few out in the floor.
- Cobwebs, and the room left dark.
- In the kin: a single fallen chair in a storeroom, a few telescopes and statues put away among castle crates, a floor lever beside the kegs, bookcases and a desk in a mine's ore store. Stores are not always pure. They collect odd leftovers, and their kegs sit in uneven heaps more than in tidy rows.
