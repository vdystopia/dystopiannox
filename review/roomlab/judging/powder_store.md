# Powder store: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign rooms of this type, described by their qualities, not room by room.

Westwood has very few powder stores, so a sheet may also show rooms of the kin store types: cellars, storerooms and treasuries. Judge each picture as a room of its own type, by its design.

## What it is for

A magazine for black powder in a keep or a mine, kept apart from the living rooms. It is a store, nothing more.

## What you notice first

The black-powder kegs, many of them in a small room.

## What it holds

- Common: powder kegs, and almost nothing else; a plain barrel or two may stand among them.
- Not seen: lights of any kind (no candelabra, no brazier), beds, tables, chairs, shelves, rugs, plants.
- In the kin stores: plain kegs, a larger cask, piled barrels, dark or steel crates, barrels of tools, a chest; a hoard's chests and sacks.

## How it is arranged

The kegs stand in knots and short rows, and some stand free of the walls, not all lined up neatly against them. The layout is close to mirror-symmetric. Westwood's cellars push their kegs into one or two corners or a tight block, with crates by the door. Storerooms mix crates and barrels along two or three walls and leave a lane.

## Density and feel

Crowded and practical, with little open floor and kegs in most of it. The cellars run from a tight block in a small room to a few kegs left in the corners of a big, mostly empty undercroft.

## Variants

Powder stores are of the town or keep style. The kin stores carry their culture's chests and crates.

## What a designer's hand looks like here

- Kegs heaped in knots, a few out in the floor.
- Cobwebs, and the room left dark.
- In the kin: a fallen chair in a store, odd things put away among the casks, a lever beside the kegs, records kept in a working store. Stores are not always pure. They collect odd leftovers, and their kegs sit in uneven heaps more than in tidy rows.
