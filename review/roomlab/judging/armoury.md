# Armoury: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign rooms of this type, described from the rooms themselves.

Westwood's campaign has only four armouries, all small (15 to 35 tiles): two in Hecubah's Dun Mir castle and two in towns. The Westwood rooms on the sheet may therefore include barracks and guardrooms, the martial kin of the armoury.

## What it is for

A garrison's arms and armour kept ready to hand: a closet of gear rather than a hall of it.

## What you notice first

A rack or two of arms standing against a back wall: a pole-arm rack (in three of the four), an armour stand, a clothes rack, a bow rack.

## What it holds

- Common: two or three racks, mixed in kind; pole arms most often.
- Sometimes: swords hung on a wall, a helm shelf, a Dun Mir chest, a pair of crates, a single barrel.
- Not seen: tables, chairs, beds, shelves of goods, sacks, a counter, hangings of décor, plants.

Only three to four kinds of thing and four to six pieces in a room.

## How it is arranged

Everything stands against the walls, mostly the two back walls; the middle is empty in three of the four. In the castle's 25-tile armoury, racks take one back wall and a helm shelf and crates the other. There are no rows of racks down the middle.

## Density and feel

Small and orderly, with a third to four fifths of the floor open. Spare, not stocked.

## Variants

Dun Mir's carry Dun Mir chests and dark crates; the town's are barer, a rack or two and a barrel or hung swords.

## The kin rooms on the sheet

- Guardrooms (eleven in the campaign): small rooms round a round table with two or three chairs, often one fallen; a chest; barrels in a corner; sometimes cots and straw; swords, shields or a bear's head on the wall; in the gaol's guardroom a bow rack, a quiver rack and pole arms.
- Barracks: beds of one kind along a wall, a table with chairs, chests, sometimes a rack or two.

## What a designer's hand looks like here

Racks of different kinds side by side; a chest pushed in beside two clothes racks; a lone barrel. Pieces sit against the nearest wall rather than evenly spaced, and nothing is symmetric.
