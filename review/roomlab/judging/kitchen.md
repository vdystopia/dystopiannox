# Kitchen: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign rooms of this type, described from the rooms themselves.

## What it is for

Where food is cooked. Westwood has only three true kitchens, two in town and one in Dun Mir. The real rooms on the sheet may well be of the kin types, a herbalist's room, a laboratory, a library, a smithy or a winch room, and should be judged as those.

## What you notice first

The iron stove. Every one of Westwood's kitchens has one, usually on a back wall (one of the two upper walls in the picture). The big Dun Mir kitchen has three stoves and two wall fireplaces. One town kitchen has a freestanding fireplace as its centre.

## What it holds

- Always: the iron stove; a table, which may be long, square, oval or round with food on it.
- Sometimes: a chair or two; barrels (nine along the walls of the big Dun Mir kitchen); a painting.
- Absent: cauldrons, sacks, apple crates, shelves of provisions, crates, food on the floor, plants and ornaments. These are working rooms with few kinds of thing, about four.

## How it is arranged

The stoves stand against the walls, and in the big kitchen they are spread over several walls rather than in one line. The tables go along a front wall or a little out from it, toward the middle. Barrels stand along the back walls and one side wall in loose groups. The middle is mostly clear, and the doors (often two or three, since a kitchen is passed through) open onto open floor.

## Density and feel

Plain, open and practical. A small town kitchen is almost bare: one stove, one oval table, one chair and a painting. The big castle kitchen is busy, with stoves, fires, long tables and barrels, and still about half of its floor is open.

## Variants

The Dun Mir kitchen is large, with long tables, many barrels and more than one fire. Town kitchens are small and spare.

## What a designer's hand looks like here

The stove sits wherever the room allows, not centred. Tables of different shapes share one room, there is a painting over a bare kitchen, and barrels are grouped where they would be used rather than spaced evenly.
