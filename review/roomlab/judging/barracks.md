# Barracks: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign rooms of this type, described by their qualities, not room by room.

Westwood's barracks are of two kinds: soldiers' rooms and ogre dens.

## What it is for

Where a crew sleeps, eats and idles together.

## What you notice first

In a soldiers' barracks, the beds: one kind to a room (wooden beds, plain beds or cots), side by side along a wall, headboards to it; a big room may run a long line of them. In an ogre den there is no single focus: heaps of straw on the floor are its commonest piece, with a crude bed or two against a back wall.

## What it holds

- Soldiers': three to six beds; a table with chairs in every one (round, oval, or a few plain tables); one or more chests. Sometimes a rack or two of arms, swords or crossed weapons hung, a desk.
- Ogre dens: three to ten straw heaps, crude beds, a crude table with a bench and stools, barrels single and piled, a water barrel, meat on the floor, a chest.
- Not seen: nightstands, bedside rugs, shelves, bookcases, plants, paintings, banners, any hearth or fire pit.

## How it is arranged

The beds stand mostly together on one wall, and the chests may share it. The table and chairs stand apart, out on the floor in the bigger rooms. Ogre dens are loose: about half their pieces sit out in the middle, straw heaps lie free with no pattern, barrels gather against a wall or two.

## Density and feel

A soldiers' room runs from crowded (about three quarters filled) to about two thirds open. Big ogre dens are sparse, wide bare floor between a few knots of things; small ones are cluttered with straw. Only four to eight kinds of thing to a room.

## Variants

Soldiers' rooms carry their culture's beds, chests and hung arms, sometimes a table on a rug. Ogre dens are straw, crude furniture, barrels and meat, with no row of beds.

## What a designer's hand looks like here

Chairs knocked over; a desk at the end of a bed row; an old table with mismatched chairs. Beyond the one kind of bed, the pieces are a mixed handful rather than matched sets, and nothing is laid out symmetrically.
