# Barracks: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign rooms of this type, described from the rooms themselves.

Westwood's campaign has seven barracks: three soldiers' rooms (one in a town keep, two in Hecubah's Dun Mir castle) and four ogre dens.

## What it is for

Where a crew sleeps, eats and idles together.

## What you notice first

In a soldiers' barracks, the beds: one kind to a room (wooden beds, plain beds or cots), side by side along a wall, headboards to it; the castle's largest has six cots in a line. In an ogre den there is no single focus: heaps of straw on the floor are its commonest piece, with a crude bed or two against a back wall.

## What it holds

- Soldiers': three to six beds; a table with chairs in every one (round, oval, or a few plain tables); one or more chests. Sometimes a bow rack and a pole-arm rack, swords or crossed weapons hung, a desk.
- Ogre dens: three to ten straw heaps, crude beds, a crude table with a bench and stools, barrels single and piled, a water barrel, meat on the floor, a chest.
- Not seen: nightstands, bedside rugs, shelves, bookcases, plants, paintings, banners, any hearth or fire pit.

## How it is arranged

The beds stand mostly together on one wall; in the castle's big barracks the chests share it. The table and chairs stand apart, out on the floor in the bigger rooms. Ogre dens are loose: about half their pieces sit out in the middle, straw heaps lie free with no pattern, barrels gather against a wall or two.

## Density and feel

The small town barracks is crowded, about three quarters filled; the castle's two are a third and three quarters open. Big ogre dens are sparse, wide bare floor between a few knots of things; small ones are cluttered with straw. Only four to eight kinds of thing to a room.

## Variants

Town (wooden beds, a bow rack, pole arms), Dun Mir (cots or beds, Dun Mir chests, hung swords, tables on rugs) and ogre (straw, crude furniture, barrels, meat; no row of beds).

## What a designer's hand looks like here

Chairs knocked over (three of the town barracks' four); a desk at the end of a bed row; an oval table with five old chairs. Beyond the one kind of bed, the pieces are a mixed handful rather than matched sets, and nothing is laid out symmetrically.
