# Workshop: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign rooms of this type, described by their qualities, not room by room.

## What it is for

A craftsman's room for making things: a wheelwright's, a carpenter's, a millwright's. Westwood's campaign has no room of this type, so a sheet may show rooms of the kin work types (a herbalist's room, a kitchen, a laboratory, a library, a smithy or a winch room). Judge each picture as a room of its own type, by its design. What follows is what a workshop is, and what Westwood's work rooms are like.

## What you notice first

In a workshop, the work bench with the job on it. In Westwood's work rooms the work piece usually stands against a wall: a laboratory's workstations, a kitchen's stove and hearth, a library's bookcases; only the smith's anvil and bellows stand out from the wall.

## What it holds

A workshop would hold a bench, tools on shelves, wheels and gears, barrels of tools and crates; no forge and no counter. Westwood's work rooms hold their trade's pieces plus a few plain ones: a desk and chair, a table (sometimes with food on it), a barrel or crate, water barrels. Its working stores show the craftsman's stock: steel crates, barrels of tools, tools, a cart.

## How it is arranged

In Westwood's work rooms most pieces stand at the walls, often three or four walls used, with the middle left open or holding one table. Bookcases and workstations run along a wall; stores sit in a knot in a corner or by a wall rather than spread out.

## Density and feel

Balanced, not crowded: typically four to fifteen kinds of thing, about a tenth of the floor covered, and half to four fifths of it open. Small work rooms are tight; big ones feel spacious.

## Variants

Westwood's work rooms appear in towns, keeps and mines; a working store is steel crates and steel barrels.

## What a designer's hand looks like here

Lived-in clutter in small doses: a meal on the table, a spittoon, a fallen chair, an apple crate, a jar, a rug by a fire. Pieces of the trade come in uneven numbers, not matched sets, and the walls are used unevenly, one heavily, another bare.
