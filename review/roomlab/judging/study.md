# Study: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign rooms of this type, described from the rooms themselves.

## What it is for

One scholar's or official's work room: a desk to write at, books, and a fire. Westwood has only three studies, all in one town map. The real rooms on the sheet may therefore be of the kin types, a bedroom, a library, a living room or a lord's chamber (solar), and should be judged as those.

## What you notice first

The desk with its chair against a back wall (one of the two upper walls in the picture), with bookcases near it.

## What it holds

- In all three: the desk and a chair, and bookcases (two, five and seven).
- In two: a hearth with bellows beside it. The third has an iron stove in a corner.
- Sometimes: a round table with four chairs, a stool.
- Absent: chests, curios, statues, plants and hangings. These are plain working rooms with few kinds of thing, about seven.

## How it is arranged

Almost everything stands against the walls and the middle is bare. Bookcases run along a back wall and also along a front wall, where they are seen from behind. They stand in a short line, often touching, rather than one here and one there. The desk takes its own stretch of back wall, the fire another wall, and the table with its chairs goes near the hearth.

## Density and feel

Quiet and fairly sparse, with about two thirds or more of the floor open. The books and the desk make the room, not ornament.

## Variants

All three are town rooms. A lord's chamber elsewhere puts a desk and bookcases at one end of a bigger room, with the bed or a long table at the other. A household room with a hearth, bookcases and a table of food is a living room, not a study.

## What a designer's hand looks like here

The bookcases are uneven: two in one room and seven in another, lining one wall and spilling onto the next. The fire is in a corner or on a side wall rather than opposite the desk, and the desk is not centred under the books.
