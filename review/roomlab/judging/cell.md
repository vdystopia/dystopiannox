# Cell: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign rooms of this type, described from the rooms themselves.

## What it is for

A room someone is locked in. Westwood's ten are three gaol cells, six ogre pens for the ogres' captives, and one barred cell block.

## What you notice first

In a gaol cell, the cot against a back wall. In an ogre pen, the heaps of straw on a bare floor. Each opens through a single barred jail door.

## What it holds

- Gaol cells (about 13 tiles): one cot and heaped bundles of straw, nine to twelve of them, and nothing else. One of the three holds only the cot.
- Ogre pens (30 to 35 tiles): four to seven heaps of ogre straw. Most also have one crude upright piece standing free in the floor, read by eye as the stocks or a primitive post. A pen sometimes has a single stool, a rough bench or a barrel.
- The cell block (about 40 tiles): iron bars along the room, skeletons in the straw, a few barrels in a corner.
- Not seen: tables, chairs (beyond an ogre's stool), chests, shelves, hangings, rugs, plants, statues, hearths. Cells have no lights of their own.

## How it is arranged

In a gaol cell the cot is against the wall farthest from the door, and the straw is heaped along one or two walls beside it. In the pens almost nothing touches the walls: the straw lies in loose heaps out in the floor, with the upright piece among them. The doorway is kept clear.

## Density and feel

Bare and hard. A gaol cell is small and fairly full, with the straw taking up much of it. A pen is mostly empty floor, about two thirds open.

## Variants

There are two kinds: the town gaol (a cot, straw, cobbled floor, a jail door) and the ogres' pens (ogre straw, crude pieces, rough stone walls, cobwebs).

## What a designer's hand looks like here

- Straw heaped unevenly and banked against one wall, not spread in an even carpet.
- Cells side by side that differ: one has straw, its neighbour has only the cot.
- A pen with one stool or one barrel, as if left behind.
- Skeletons lying in the straw of the cell block.
