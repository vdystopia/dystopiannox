# Solar: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign rooms of this type, described from the rooms themselves.

## What it is for

A lord's own chamber, larger than a bedroom: one end for sleeping, the other for sitting and receiving. Westwood has only four, so the real rooms on the sheet may be bedrooms, living rooms or studies (see below).

## What you notice first

The size, and the two ends. In the two town chambers the hearth anchors the sitting end, centred on a wall between bookcases with tables and chairs before it; the bed lies at the other end, once in an alcove of its own. In Dun Mir's chambers the sitting end is a long table with benches, and a pool, columns and statues stand in the room.

## What it holds

- One bed (one Dun Mir chamber has two; a wizard's has a humble cot), a nightstand in two.
- Bookcases in three, ten or more in one; chests; tables with chairs; benches.
- A bearskin before the bed or hearth in three of the four; a red rug.
- Tapestries of one or two colours, sometimes ten of them; candelabras.
- Seen once: a desk, columns, statues, obelisks.
- Not in Westwood's: plants, or the working pieces of a kitchen or laboratory.

## How it is arranged

The room's shape does the zoning: an L-shape, an alcove, a partition across the middle. Bookcases line a wall in runs; the rest stands in groups, and in Dun Mir's big chambers almost everything stands free of the walls. Open floor separates the two ends.

## Density and feel

Rich but roomy: twelve to twenty kinds of thing over 100 to 200 tiles, with about two thirds of the floor open. Some are carpeted wall to wall.

## Variants

Town chambers: hearth, bookcases, tapestries. Dun Mir's: columns, a pool, benches at long tables, Dun Mir chests.

## What a designer's hand looks like here

Lived-in oddities: water barrels by the wall, a steel crate, an open chest, a fallen chair, a spittoon, bellows at the hearth. Colours repeat (one room's tapestries are all green).

## Kin rooms

Bedrooms are small (about 9 to 45 tiles): the bed in a corner with a nightstand, a bookcase or two, a chest, often a desk and chair. Living rooms centre on a hearth or stove, a round table with chairs on a rug, bookcases. Studies put a desk among bookcases, sometimes with a hearth or stove.
