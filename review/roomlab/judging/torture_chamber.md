# Torture chamber: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign rooms of this type, described from the rooms themselves.

Westwood's campaign has no room it treats as a torture chamber. The real rooms on this sheet are most likely its kin, Westwood's cells: gaol cells and ogre pens. Judge them as cells.

## What it is for

A question room under a keep or in a dungeon, where prisoners are hurt for answers.

## What you notice first

The few dungeon rooms in Westwood's campaign that come near the idea have a torture rack standing free in the floor, or a body's remains lying on it.

## What it holds

- In those dungeon rooms: a rack, a body's remains, a chest, a candelabra, empty shelves. The smaller one has only remains and a chest.
- In the kin cells: a cot and heaped straw in a small gaol cell; heaps of ogre straw and a crude upright piece (stocks or a primitive post) in an ogre pen; bars, skeletons in straw and a few barrels in a cell block.
- Not seen in any of them: tables of food, beds with bedding, rugs, plants, hangings, statues, altars.

## How it is arranged

Westwood's rack rooms keep their few pieces apart, with the rack out in the floor and a chest or shelves at a wall. In the cells the cot is against the far wall with straw heaped beside it, while the ogre pens leave their straw in loose heaps away from the walls.

## Density and feel

Very bare: in the rack rooms almost all the floor is open, and the cells are about half to three quarters open. These are cold, hard rooms of iron, wood and straw, where every piece stands alone.

## Variants

No culture variants in the campaign beyond the ogres' pens (rough straw and crude pieces).

## What a designer's hand looks like here

- Remains left on the floor instead of an arranged set of instruments.
- Shelves standing empty.
- A pen with one stool or one barrel, a cell holding only its cot.
- Very few pieces, unevenly placed: Westwood's rooms of this kind look abandoned more than staged.
