# Winch room: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign rooms of this type, described by their qualities, not room by room.

## What it is for

The machine room behind a lift, a gate or a mine's elevator: the gears and pulleys that drive it, and the stores of the men who keep it running. Westwood's are in mines and under keeps.

## What you notice first

The machinery: gear trains and great pulley gears set round the walls, and in some rooms the lift itself or its pit in the middle. The machinery is the room; the furniture hardly registers.

## What it holds

- Gear trains and pulley gears, often many of them; small gears.
- Barrels of tools in every one; steel crates and plain crates in most; plain barrels.
- Sometimes: a chest, a stool, braziers or furnaces, a cart's wheel, buttons on the wall, cobwebs.
- Not in Westwood's: beds, tables, chairs, desks, shelves, hearths, rugs, plants.

## How it is arranged

The gears and pulleys hug the walls, round the room. The few stores sit by a wall, a barrel of tools beside a crate, in one or two small knots rather than lined up. The middle is open floor, or the lift. Pieces are spread over several walls, none of them lined.

## Density and feel

Almost empty: a handful of kinds of furnishing besides the machinery, with nearly all the floor bare. Mechanical and raw; some are cut in the rock.

## Variants

Each culture brings its own chest and barrels; otherwise crates and barrels everywhere.

## What a designer's hand looks like here

The room is shaped by the machine, not by furnishing: a big bare floor with gears massed on the walls, a lift in the middle, lights in the corners. The stores are few and offhand, a crate and a tool barrel dropped by a wall, perhaps a single stool.
