# Winch room: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign rooms of this type, described from the rooms themselves.

## What it is for

The machine room behind a lift, a gate or a mine's elevator: the gears and pulleys that drive it, and the stores of the men who keep it running. Westwood has four, in the mines and under a keep. With so few, the real rooms on the sheet may also be of the kin work types (a herbalist's room, a kitchen, a laboratory, a library or a smithy).

## What you notice first

The machinery: gear trains and great pulley gears set round the walls, and in some rooms the lift itself or its pit in the middle. The machinery is the room; the furniture hardly registers.

## What it holds

- Gear trains and pulley gears, often many of them; small gears.
- Barrels of tools in all four; steel crates and plain crates in three; plain barrels.
- Seen once: a Dun Mir chest, a stool, braziers or furnaces in the corners, an ore cart's wheel, buttons on the wall.
- Cobwebs in the mine's.
- Not in Westwood's: beds, tables, chairs, desks, shelves, hearths, rugs, plants.

## How it is arranged

The gears and pulleys hug the walls, round the room. The few stores sit by a wall, a barrel of tools beside a crate, in one or two small knots rather than lined up. The middle is open floor, or the lift. Pieces are spread over several walls, none of them lined.

## Density and feel

Almost empty: three to nine kinds of furnishing besides the machinery in rooms of about 35 to 180 tiles, with nearly all the floor bare. Mechanical and raw; some are cut in the rock.

## Variants

Two are Dun Mir's: a Dun Mir chest, steel barrels. The town ones: plain and steel crates, barrels.

## What a designer's hand looks like here

The room is shaped by the machine, not by furnishing: a big bare floor with gears massed on the walls, a lift in the middle, braziers in the corners of the rock-cut one. The stores are few and offhand, a crate and a tool barrel dropped by a wall; one room adds a single stool.
