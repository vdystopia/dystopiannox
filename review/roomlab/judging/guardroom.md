# Guardroom: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign rooms of this type, described by their qualities, not room by room.

## What it is for

Where a watch waits by the door it guards: a gaol's guard room, a watch hut, a keep's guard room or a dungeon guard post. Most are small, with a few bigger ones.

## What you notice first

A table with chairs round it. In about three quarters of them it is a round table, now and then with a meal on it. Now and then the table gives way to cots, chests and straw.

## What it holds

- Common: the table and two to four chairs, often mismatched (dark, old and plain chairs together). Barrels stand by the walls in most. About half have a chest.
- Sometimes: a cot or two (in about a third), arms hung on a wall (swords or shields), a bench, a second smaller table, a spittoon, a trophy, straw, a rug under the table.
- Rare: racks of weapons.
- Not seen: bars, counters, altars, hearths, bookcases, plants.

## How it is arranged

In the small posts nearly everything is pushed to the walls: barrels in a corner or two, the chest against a wall, cots along a wall, and chairs sometimes backed against a wall instead of drawn up to the table. The table sits in the middle or off to one side. Several have two or three doorways, with the way across left open.

## Density and feel

Lived in and a little untidy: about half the floor open, few pieces crowding a small room.

## Variants

Each culture brings its own chests, chairs and hung arms; the rest is barrels and a table everywhere.

## What a designer's hand looks like here

- A chair knocked over on the floor.
- Odd chairs that do not match, and a small table beside a round one.
- Rooms that are rarely symmetric: barrels bunched in one corner, a single arms piece on one wall, the cots both on one side.
