# Herbalist: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign rooms of this type, described by their qualities, not room by room.

## What it is for

An herb-lore and remedy room: brewing, books of remedies, jars and herbs. It is homelier and earthier than a wizard's laboratory.

## What you notice first

The potion shelves, standing against a back wall (one of the two upper walls in the picture). A desk with its chair is the other constant.

## What it holds

- Common: potion shelves (one, or a pair); a desk with a chair; a table with chairs.
- Sometimes: bookcases, shelves of herbs and jars, an alchemist's desk, a fairy jar or two, a fire with its bellows, a rug before it, and a stray piece of stock (a barrel, a crate, an apple crate) or a spittoon.
- Absent: a cauldron, plants or pots of herbs, sacks along the walls, hangings and statues.

## How it is arranged

The shelving and the desk take the back walls. Bookcases may stand in a line along a front wall (the lower walls nearest the viewer), seen from behind. The table with its chairs stands a little out into the floor. A single crate or barrel sits by a wall as stock. Where there is a fire, it may stand a little out from the wall with its tools and a rug before it.

## Density and feel

Homely and moderately full, with many kinds of thing (about ten) but each one once or twice. About two thirds of the floor is open.

## Variants

Some lean to the hearth (a fire, a rug, jars), others to study (books, shelves of herbs, an alchemist's desk).

## What a designer's hand looks like here

Odd domestic pieces sit among the remedies: a spittoon, a rug by the fire, a lone barrel or crate. The shelving is of mixed kinds, and nothing is mirrored or evenly spaced.
