# Herbalist: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign rooms of this type, described from the rooms themselves.

## What it is for

An herb-lore and remedy room: brewing, books of remedies, jars and herbs. It is homelier and earthier than a wizard's laboratory. Westwood has only two. The real rooms on the sheet may be laboratories, the kin type, and should be judged as those.

## What you notice first

The potion shelves, the one piece both rooms share, standing against a back wall (one of the two upper walls in the picture). A desk with its chair is the other constant.

## What it holds

- In both: potion shelves (one, or a pair); a desk; a table with chairs (square in one, round in the other).
- In one room: five bookcases, two full shelves of herbs and jars, an alchemist's desk, a barrel and a dark crate.
- In the other: two fairy jars, a freestanding fireplace with its bellows, a bearskin rug, an apple crate and a spittoon.
- Absent from both: a cauldron, plants or pots of herbs, sacks along the walls, hangings and statues.

## How it is arranged

The shelving and the desk take the back walls. In one room the bookcases stand in a line along a front wall (the lower walls nearest the viewer), seen from behind. The table with its chairs stands a little out into the floor. A single crate, barrel or apple crate sits by a wall as stock. In the Dun Mir room the fire stands a little out from the wall with its bellows and the bearskin before it.

## Density and feel

Homely and moderately full, with many kinds of thing (about eleven) but each one once or twice. About two thirds of the floor is open.

## Variants

One, in Dun Mir, keeps a fire, a rug and fairy jars. The other is given over to books, shelves of herbs and an alchemist's desk.

## What a designer's hand looks like here

Odd domestic pieces sit among the remedies: a spittoon, a bearskin by the fire, an apple crate, a lone barrel. The shelving is of mixed kinds, and nothing is mirrored or evenly spaced.
