# Laboratory: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign rooms of this type, described from the rooms themselves.

## What it is for

A wizard's workroom for experiments, brewing and reading. Westwood has six, all town rooms, ranging from a closet to a great hall.

## What you notice first

The wizard's workstations, the tall benches of apparatus. All six labs have them, from one to five each, and they are the focal piece. They stand against the walls, often the front walls (the lower walls nearest the viewer, seen from behind), and some sit in corners.

## What it holds

- Common: workstations (all six); a desk with a chair (five of six).
- In the bigger rooms: bookcases (four in one, twelve in another), a round table laid with food and its chairs, and a long work table.
- Once each: four small tesla coils, an alchemist's desk, a pair of gargoyles, a vase, two blue tapestries, a painting, a red rug, a fairy jar, shelves of jars, and a barrel and crate.
- Absent: cauldrons, plants, candle circles and work islands in the middle.

## How it is arranged

The room is worked along its walls and the middle is left as open floor. Workstations take one or two walls and the desk another, often a back wall (one of the two upper walls in the picture). In the large rooms, bookcases run along the walls, front walls included. The tesla coils stand together along one wall. A table with food sits off to one side.

## Density and feel

Fairly open, with about three fifths of the floor clear. The smallest is four workstations in a closet. The great ones fill their walls with apparatus and books but keep the floor free. They are lit more brightly than most rooms.

## Variants

All six are town rooms. A room with an alchemist's desk and a bed is a bedroom, and Galava's museum of glass cases is a gallery.

## What a designer's hand looks like here

The apparatus is of mixed kinds, unevenly grouped and sometimes on the front walls. A meal is left on a table in a workroom, and a barrel and crate sit in a small one. The six rooms differ a great deal from one another, with no set layout.
