# Ossuary: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign rooms of this type, described from the rooms themselves.

Westwood's campaign has no room it treats as an ossuary, so the real rooms on this sheet will be its kin: crypts and mausoleums. Judge them as Westwood's rooms of the dead.

## What it is for

The bone room of a catacomb, where the bones go once the graves are full.

## What you notice first

In Westwood's catacombs a few side rooms come close. Their bones lie in heaps on a bare floor, with a crypt chest or two against the walls. One tiny niche holds a crypt chest and about twenty bones.

## What it holds

- In those bone rooms: skulls and bones, one or two crypt chests, sometimes a pair of monuments.
- In the kin crypts: sarcophagi, usually in pairs, sometimes four or six; tombstones; crypt chests; an obelisk or a few statues.
- In the kin mausoleums: a sarcophagus with statues of the dead round it, monuments, a crypt chest, columns in one.
- Not seen in any of them: beds, tables, chairs, benches, shelves, barrels, sacks, straw, rugs, plants.

## How it is arranged

Crypt chests stand against the walls and bones are heaped on the floor. Westwood's crypts are almost always mirror-symmetric: sarcophagi in matched pairs down the middle of the room, tombstones in matched sets, and nothing hung on the walls. Many have doorways on two or more sides, with a clear way between the tombs.

## Density and feel

Bare and quiet. Most of the floor is open (in a typical crypt about three quarters), and few kinds of thing appear, often only two. Rooms range from a 9-tile niche to about 90 tiles. Lighting is dim and sparse.

## Variants

All Westwood's crypts and bone rooms are in the town style (grey stone, crypt chests, tombstones). The Land of the Dead has bones lying in many of its rooms, with dark tombstones and mana obelisks.

## What a designer's hand looks like here

- Bones heaped in clusters, not spread evenly; many bones crowded into the smallest niche.
- Twin rooms repeated along a catacomb, each a copy of the next.
- In a few small crypts the symmetry breaks: a single sarcophagus with one tombstone set off to one side.
