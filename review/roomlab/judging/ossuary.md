# Ossuary: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign rooms of this type, described by their qualities, not room by room.

Westwood's campaign has no room it treats as an ossuary, so a sheet may show rooms of the kin types: crypts and mausoleums. Judge each picture as a room of its own type, by its design.

## What it is for

The bone room of a catacomb, where the bones go once the graves are full.

## What you notice first

In Westwood's catacombs a few side rooms come close. Their bones lie in heaps on a bare floor, with a crypt chest or two against the walls; even a tiny niche may be crowded with bones round a chest.

## What it holds

- In those bone rooms: skulls and bones, one or two crypt chests, sometimes a pair of monuments.
- In the kin crypts: sarcophagi, usually in pairs or short rows; tombstones; crypt chests; now and then statues or tall stones.
- In the kin mausoleums: a sarcophagus with statues of the dead round it, monuments, a crypt chest, sometimes columns.
- Not seen in any of them: beds, tables, chairs, benches, shelves, barrels, sacks, straw, rugs, plants.

## How it is arranged

Crypt chests stand against the walls and bones are heaped on the floor. Westwood's crypts are almost always mirror-symmetric: sarcophagi in matched pairs down the middle of the room, tombstones in matched sets, and nothing hung on the walls. Many have doorways on two or more sides, with a clear way between the tombs.

## Density and feel

Bare and quiet. Most of the floor is open (in a typical crypt about three quarters), and few kinds of thing appear, often only two. Rooms range from tiny niches to modest chambers. Lighting is dim and sparse.

## Variants

Westwood's crypts and bone rooms are of one grey-stone style (crypt chests, tombstones). Bones also lie about in the rooms of its darker places, with dark stones among them.

## What a designer's hand looks like here

- Bones heaped in clusters, not spread evenly; many bones crowded into the smallest niche.
- Twin rooms repeated along a catacomb, each a copy of the next.
- In a few small crypts the symmetry breaks: a single sarcophagus with one tombstone set off to one side.
