# Storeroom: what a real one is like

For the blind judge: Westwood's campaign rooms of this type, described from the rooms themselves.

## What it is for

Where a house, keep, mine or castle keeps its supplies. Westwood has seven, five in town and two in Dun Mir. They range from closets of a few tiles to a castle's large store.

## What you notice first

There is no single focal piece. You see heaps of barrels and crates against the walls, usually two of them.

## What it holds

- Common: barrels in every room, plain in most and otherwise steel-banded, tool, water or piled barrels; crates (plain, dark or steel) in most; apple crates in two.
- Sometimes: a chest (Dun Mir chests in both Dun Mir stores); great casks.
- In single rooms: six shelves of jars in a pantry closet; bookcases, a table and a chair in a mine's store; telescopes, statues and gargoyles put away among casks in a castle's store; a fallen chair.
- Typically absent: lights, beds, hearths, rugs, plants and hangings. Most stores hold only three or four kinds of thing.

## How it is arranged

Most rooms have stores against two walls, and the other walls are bare. Pieces cluster: barrels touch in twos and threes, a crate is pushed in beside them, and a heap fills a corner. In larger rooms a knot of barrels or a stack of crates stands out on the floor near the heaps, with an aisle to walk in. A closet may be crammed nearly full.

## Density and feel

In order but uneven. The tiny closets are packed. The bigger stores are about half open floor, with the stock bunched rather than spread out.

## Variants

Dun Mir stores keep a Dun Mir chest by their barrels. A mine's store has steel crates, tool barrels and bookcases of records. A castle's store becomes a lumber room for statues and instruments. Kegs alone make a cellar, and black-powder kegs make a powder store.

## What a designer's hand looks like here

Mixed kinds share a heap, and some things are plainly put away, such as telescopes stood among casks or a chair knocked over. One corner is piled high while the opposite wall is bare, and there are no tidy rows.
